Figure 1: Binding Energy per Nucleon Curve

Shape:

Peaks near A = 56 (iron/nickel region) at ~8.8 MeV/nucleon, rises steeply from hydrogen, and gradually decreases for heavy nuclei (A > 56).

Significance:

  • Nuclei near the peak (Fe-56) are the most stable — maximum binding energy per nucleon.
  • Fission: Heavy nuclei (A ~ 235) have lower binding energy/nucleon (~7.6 MeV). Splitting them releases energy because products have higher binding energy/nucleon.
  • Fusion: Light nuclei (H, D, T) have very low binding energy/nucleon. Fusing them releases energy for the same reason.
  • The curve explains why both fission and fusion release energy.

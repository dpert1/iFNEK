Figure 6: Operating Regions of Ionizing Detectors

Shape:

Log-linear plot of pulse amplitude vs. applied voltage. Six named regions with two curves (alpha and beta) that converge and plateau.

Significance:

  • Recombination region: Voltage too low; ions recombine before collection — not useful for detection.
  • Ionization chamber region: All primary ions collected; pulse proportional to energy deposited. Alpha and beta pulses differ in size (distinguishable).
  • Proportional region: Gas multiplication amplifies signal proportionally; can still distinguish radiation types by pulse height.
  • Limited proportionality: Space charge distorts response; transitional.
  • Geiger-Mueller (GM) region: Full avalanche; all pulses same size regardless of radiation type or energy. Counts only — cannot distinguish.
  • Continuous discharge: Too high voltage; detector damaged.
